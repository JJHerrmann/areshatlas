# One Root, Five Hands

How every people in Areshnaat came to write, and how each hand descends from the
elf script. Tags: **canon** = the author decided it, **proposed** = a suggestion
awaiting a yes, **open** = undecided.

## The five hands

| Hand | Direction | Form | Derivation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elf | Vertical, top to bottom, columns left to right | Spine with ticks and loops; vowels as full letters | Root | canon |
| Dwarf | Right to left | Joined baseline, flowing, Farsi-leaning; tense in the stroke | Elf column turned 90 degrees, vowels demoted to marks | canon |
| Goblinoid | Not set | Futhark-like, straight staves | Spine broken into staves, curves straightened | canon |
| Halfling and gnome | Not set | Thaana-like, short hooks, vowel marks | Spine broken into hooks, ticks kept as marks | canon |
| Human | Left to right (proposed) | Latinized Thaana, fewer accents | Small-folk marks promoted back to letters | canon |

## Who writes with which hand

Five hands, more than five peoples. A people can borrow a hand without being
descended from it.

| People | Hand used | Note | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elves | Elf | The root hand | canon |
| Sea elves, wood elves | Elf | The same elvish script, unchanged | canon |
| Drow | **Dwarf** | Elvish words written in the dwarven hand, with adjustments for spelling | canon |
| Dwarves | Dwarf | — | canon |
| Goblinoids | Goblinoid | Futhark-like runes | canon |
| Orcs | Goblinoid | The same runes | canon |
| Halflings, gnomes | Halfling and gnome | One shared hand | canon |
| Humans | Human | A Latin script | canon |

- **[canon]** The drow are the exception to the whole descent tree. They are elf-lineage but write with the dwarven hand, carrying elvish words in it with spelling adjustments. Every other people uses the hand it inherited.
- **[open]** How the drow came to take a dwarven hand, and when. Nothing fixes it, and it is the most interesting unexplained thing in the writing canon.
- **[open]** Whether the drow adjustments amount to a sixth hand in practice, or stay a spelling convention inside the dwarven one.

## What is decided

- **[canon]** Sea elves and wood elves write the same elvish script, unchanged.
- **[canon]** The elves wrote first. Every other people's script descends from the elf hand, a vertical script in the Mongolian manner: a spine running top to bottom, with ticks and loops off either side, columns read left to right.
- **[canon]** Elves write vowels as full letters.
- **[canon]** Dwarves write tense in the stroke of the hand: present is the baseline, past is thicker, future leans at a cant of about 5 degrees, timeless gets a curled cap, and verbs are always capped. The dwarf hand leans toward Farsi in flow, and carving a flowing hand into stone is a flex expected of dwarven craftsmen.
- **[canon]** Refugees have added helper marks in daily writing, for both vowel and tense.
- **[canon]** Goblinoids use something in the manner of futhark. Orcs use the same runes.
- **[canon]** Halflings and gnomes share a Thaana-style script.
- **[canon]** Humans use a Latin script. It is described elsewhere as a Latinized Thaana, with Latin-leaning letterforms and fewer accents.

## How one hand becomes another

- **[proposed]** The dwarf hand is the elf column turned 90 degrees clockwise: top-to-bottom reading becomes right-to-left, the spine becomes the joined baseline, and the side ticks fold into marks above and below.
- **[proposed]** Corrected elf ticks: left of the spine for the deep vowels (a, o, u), right of the spine for the forward vowels (e, i). Turned clockwise, left lands above and right lands below, which matches the refugee marks, so the deep and forward split in [[_Karakeel (Dwarven)]] would be inherited from the elf script.
- **[proposed]** Goblinoid runes are the elf spine and ticks with the spine broken into separate staves and every curve straightened for carving.
- **[proposed]** The small folk keep the ticks as vowel marks and break the spine into short, separate hooks.
- **[proposed]** The small folk write left to right, so they look distinct from the dwarves at a glance. If right to left, they keep unjoined letters instead.
- **[proposed]** Humans took the script from their halfling and gnome neighbours, promoted the marks back into full vowel letters, and write left to right. The precedent is real: the Greeks turned spare Phoenician consonant signs into vowels.
- **[open]** Whether humans keep a few unmistakably Thaana-derived consonant shapes, so Common does not read as English in a funny font. This was proposed, but "a Latin script" may mean the Thaana inheritance is not visible on the page. The two readings differ and the difference is worth settling.
- **[proposed]** Halflings write loose and casual. Gnomes over-annotate, adding extra marks.
- **[proposed]** Real-world analogy: Syriac led to Sogdian, then Uyghur, which was turned on its side to become vertical Mongolian. This is a fact about real scripts, offered as precedent, not as canon.

## The dwarven alphabet

Three drafts were made of the same alphabet. Draft 1 was Cyrillic-leaning and
blocky, draft 2 leaned Farsi, and draft 3 keeps the Farsi lean and adds the two
letters the earlier drafts were missing. **Rook chose the Farsi-leaning hand, and
draft 3 is the one in use.**

- **[canon]** 20 letters, written right to left, joined along a baseline. Consonants only; the vowels are carried by the refugee helper marks.
- **[canon]** The inventory: b, p, t, n, y, h, kh, r, z, d, g, s, sh, ts, k, l, m, w, dz, and a glottal stop.
- **[canon]** `dz` is a ligature: a d stem rising from the baseline, with the z tail and its dot beside it. It is the letter every Kord seat leans on, since the blood marker `dza` appears in all of them.
- **[canon]** `sh` is the hacek letter, drawn as a dotted s. Three teeth carry s, sh and ts, and a dot above the teeth makes the hacek, keeping sh apart from s.
- **[canon]** The tooth carries b, p, t, n, y by dot count and position. The hanging bowl carries h and kh. The tail carries r and z. The hooked stroke carries d and g.
- **[canon]** Helper marks for vowels sit above the line for the deep class and below for the forward class: a is one stroke above, o a ring above, u two strokes above, e one stroke below, i two strokes below. Tense takes its own sign at the start of the word.
- **[canon]** Both Kord names that the earlier drafts could not write are now spellable, and draft 3 renders them: **Sahdzakoda** (Gold, from zahav, sa-h-dza-ko-da) and **Šohdzakoda** (Onyx, with the dotted s). See [[Dwarves and Duergar]].

## Open questions

- **[open]** Does anyone in the world know the dwarf hand descends from the elf script? The dwarves would deny it.
- **[open]** What script do the duergar use, and does it break a rule the surface dwarves keep?
- **[open]** Are the refugee helper marks new symbols, or extensions of the Kord marks?
- **[open]** Which direction do the small folk write, and do goblinoids read in rows or columns?
- **[open]** Reading direction for the goblinoid and the halfling-gnome hands is unset.
- **[open]** The five-hand family tree drawing has not been produced. The elf hand redraw has arrived, as `elf-hand.svg`, with three candidates in `elf-scripts.svg`.
- **[open]** Whether the vowel shaping that separates dwarven, halfling and gnome speech shows up in their hands as well, or only in the sound.
