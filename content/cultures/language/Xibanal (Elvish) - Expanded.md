# High Elven Naming Canon
# Xibanal

A compact naming framework for elven place names using:

- Hebrew triconsonantal roots as semantic cores
- Nahuatl-style suffixes for place and people forms
- Greek and Latin-style prestige affixes for imperial, civic, and ceremonial names

## Usage Pattern

Base pattern:

`[optional prestige prefix] + [surface form of Hebrew root] + [suffix]`

Examples:

- `Xal` + `hoph` + `tlan` -> `Xalhophtlan`
- `Tol` + `nahar` + `co` -> `Tolnaharco`
- `Aure` + `malak` + `ion` -> `Auremalakion`

## Sound Notes

Xibanal names should prefer:

- smoother vowels and brighter open syllables
- selective x, z, c, and tl flavoring where useful
- sacred or prestige layering on major states and holy sites
- elegant but still map-usable forms rather than six-syllable cathedral accidents

## Nahuatl Suffix Guide for Elvish Surface Forms

|Suffix|Core function|Best use in this project|Notes|
|---|---|---|---|
|-co|locative, place associated with X|Use for generic places, cities, shrines, ports, landmarks, and sites tied to a feature.|Broad and flexible. Good default place suffix when no narrower option is better.|
|-can|place, settled locality|Use for inhabited places such as towns, villages, districts, and established settlements.|Slightly more settlement-oriented than -co. Good for living communities.|
|-pan|on, upon, over, at the surface of|Use for places on ridges, banks, plateaus, roadsides, river edges, terraces, and surface-linked locations.|Best when the geography feels on top of or along something.|
|-tlan|place of abundance, among, near many X|Use for regions, provinces, forests, marshes, plains, and lands characterized by abundance of a thing.|Good for broad landscapes and culturally defined regions rather than single cities.|
|-yan|place of action, place where X happens|Use for markets, courts, crossings, workshops, forges, ritual precincts, and other function-defined places.|Strong choice when a site is known for what is done there, not just what is there.|
|-tepec|hill or mountain place|Use for hill towns, mountain cities, cliff forts, ridge shrines, and highland polities.|Good for elevated settlements and names centered on a prominent height.|
|-tecatl|person of, inhabitant of, one associated with X|Use for singular demonyms, ethnonyms, origin-titles, or lineage epithets.|Best for a person, not a place.|
|-teca|people of, collective group associated with X|Use for collective demonyms, peoples, culture labels, and group identities.|Best for the whole people, culture, or originating population.|
|-yotl|abstract state, condition, quality|Use for abstractions, virtues, offices, philosophies, conditions, and named concepts.|Not a place suffix. Good for lexical and ideological terms.|
|-li / -tl|absolutive-style noun ending|Use when coining standalone lexical nouns or formal nominal forms, not as a default place suffix.|Structural noun endings in Nahuatl-style morphology, not decorative finishing touches.|

### Practical shorthand

- Use -co when you just need a solid place name.
    
- Use -can when it is clearly a settled inhabited place.
    
- Use -pan when the place sits on, over, or along a feature.
    
- Use -tlan for broad regions or lands rich in something.
    
- Use -yan when the place is defined by function or repeated activity.
    
- Use -tepec for high places.
    
- Use -tecatl and -teca for people, not settlements.
    
- Use -yotl for ideas, states, or qualities.
    
- Use -li / -tl for coined nouns, not map names by default.
    

## Elvish Consonant Mutation Rules

These apply after the root has been surfaced with vowels and after Nahuatl-style suffixes are attached.

### Vowel behavior

- Vh always produces a short vowel.
    
- Final vowels become short for most speakers.
    

### Boundary and cluster mutations

- ch + ch -> ch (optional)
    
- ch + tz -> tz (optional)
    
- kw + p -> kp
    
- l + tl -> ll
    
- l + y -> ll
    
- m# -> n#
    
- mC -> nC unless C = m or p
    
- n + y -> yy
    
- t# -> h# in speech, though often still written t
    
- tz + ch -> ch (optional)
    
- tz + tz -> tz (optional)
    
- uc + c -> cc
    
- uc + o -> co
    
- uh + m -> mm (optional)
    
- uh + o -> o
    
- uh + p -> pp (optional)
    
- y# -> x#
    
- z + ch -> zz (usual)
    
- z + l -> zz (usual)
    
- z + t -> xt
    
- z + tz -> zz (usual)
    
- z + x -> zz (usual)
    
- z + y -> zz (usual)

## Asterisk Notes

- `*` Aganco is the default generic basin-place form. A hydrological region or enclosed basin-land could later favor `Agantlan`.
    
- `*2` Aganco is the default generic lake-place form. A lake settlement might later favor `Agancan`, while a lake-region could favor `Agantlan`.
    
- `*3` Mayanco is the default generic spring-place form. A sacred or functional spring site could later favor `Mayanyan`.
    
- `*4` Beryan treats the well as a functional site, meaning a place defined by drawing or using water. A generic place reading could later be `Berco`.
    
- `*5` Telomco is the default generic deep-place form. If the feature is treated as a process zone, trench, or dangerous active site, `Telomyan` may fit better.
    
- `*6` Xangalyan treats surf as an activity-defined littoral zone. A simpler landmark reading could later be `Xangalco`.
    
- `*7` Tehomco is the default generic abyss-place form. A broader dread-region or mythic deep could later favor `Tehomtlan`.
    
- `*8` Aficpan treats channel/riverbed as a feature one moves along or on. A more neutral place reading could later be `Aficco`, while a crossing-work site could favor `Aficyan`.
